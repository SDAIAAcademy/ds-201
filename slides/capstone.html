How the capstone is scored

The rubric

Question and estimand
15%
Intervals and coverage
25%
Tests and effect sizes
25%
Regression and limits
20%
Reproducibility
15%
Every number comes with its interval and its unit. An estimate without an interval is a guess, and an interval without a stated resampling unit cannot be checked.

Full marks, one example each. Estimand: one sentence with the quantity, the population and the groups. Intervals: the method's coverage counted in a simulation and reported in the README. Tests: the p-value with the effect size set against a stated threshold, and the number of comparisons made. Regression and limits: a slope in units with what is held fixed, or, where the question has no regression, the limits of the design stated with their direction. Reproducibility: the notebook reruns from the raw file with the seed and gives the numbers of the brief.

Format

8 minutes for the defence, then 4 minutes of questions

1 · The question

The manager's words, then the estimand in one sentence. One slide or one cell.

2 · One result

The estimate, its interval, the test and the effect size against the threshold. Rerun it live with the seed.

3 · Limits

The population, the resampling unit and why, the coverage check, what the data cannot tell.

In the questions, the assessor names a different resampling unit. You predict what happens to your interval, then run it and show it.

The prediction comes before the run and is scored on its reasoning, not on luck: wider or narrower, and why. No assistant during the defence or the questions. Peer feedback after each defence: one strength, one question, one suggestion.

Before the first defence

Final prep · 20 minutes

Rerun the notebook from the raw file, check that the numbers match the brief, and choose the one result you will defend.

Press T to start the timer. Breaks are not slides: press B when the room needs one. A notebook that gives different numbers on the rerun is the most common loss of marks; a fixed seed prevents it.
